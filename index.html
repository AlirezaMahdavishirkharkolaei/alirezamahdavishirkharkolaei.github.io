Who I am

Law, global economics and technology

I am a legal researcher and advisor with an academic and practical background in international trade law, tax policy and regulatory compliance. I hold an LL.M. from Cyprus International University, where my thesis examined the legal and economic implications of tax havens and their impact on global trade governance.

My research explores the intersection of fiscal transparency, corporate regulation and international legal frameworks, with a particular focus on coherence within the global trading system and the relationship between WTO and EU law.

Professionally, I serve as a part-time Legal Advisor at the Consulate of the Islamic Republic of Iran in Northern Cyprus, working on legal matters involving international regulations and policy interpretation. Earlier roles as a Research Assistant and Legal Assistant strengthened both my analytical and practical legal skills.

I also build software. Using Python and data analysis, I develop open-source tools that make legal reasoning explicit and verifiable, from WTO services-trade analysis to tax-risk screening and contract review. I aim to work where law, technology and the global economy meet.

Open source

Legal-tech projects

Research tools that apply legal frameworks transparently. Each one runs in the browser, is tested, and cites the legal basis of its output.

WTO law · GATS

WTO–GATS Reasoning Engine

Takes a regulatory measure through the GATS analytical sequence developed in WTO jurisprudence. It produces a reasoning trace with the provisions and disputes behind each step, and it reports missing facts as information gaps instead of guessing them.

Open the tool →Code

International tax · Pillar Two

TaxRiskScan

Analyses Country-by-Country Reporting data to flag jurisdictions with signs of profit shifting or low taxation, and screens them for Pillar Two minimum-tax exposure. It combines transparent rules, each linked to its legal basis, with a classifier trained on real public CbCRs.

Open the tool →Code

Rules as code

Legal-to-Code

A research framework that classifies rules of international economic law by how far they can be computed, then implements the computable ones as tested, traceable code. Coverage includes the OECD Model Tax Convention and the WTO dispute settlement timeline.

Open the tool →Code

Contracts · AI

LexScan

A contract review workspace with an instant clause scan in English, Turkish and Greek, and an AI review in which every quoted passage is checked against the document. The interface is available in six languages.

Scholarly work

Publications & research

  1. Preprint · Cambridge Open Engage · 2026

    From Fiscal to Legal Fragmentation: Coherence Challenges in Global Trade Governance

    Argues that tax havens are not a mere fiscal anomaly but a symptom of a deeper institutional crisis in the fragmentation of international trade governance. Through doctrinal and comparative analysis of tax, trade and environmental regimes, it draws a parallel between the loss of coherence in global tax law and the growing divergence between the WTO and the EU, and proposes coherence as a preventive principle of international economic law.

    Tax havens · Fiscal fragmentation · Legal fragmentation · International trade governance · WTO–EU relations

    Read the preprint →

  2. Preprint · Cambridge Open Engage · 2026

    The Legal and Technological Implications of Tax Havens: Challenges to Corporate Transparency and Open Access to Fiscal Information

    Examines how the opacity of offshore financial systems undermines corporate transparency and public access to fiscal information in the post-BEPS era. Drawing on the Panama Papers and Country-by-Country Reporting, it analyses the tension between corporate confidentiality and the public interest, and recommends legal and technological mechanisms to harmonise international norms and strengthen accountability.

    Tax havens · Fiscal transparency · BEPS · Open access to legal information · Corporate tax disclosure

    Read the preprint →

  3. LL.M. thesis · Cyprus International University · 2025

    The Impact of Tax Havens on International Trade Law

    Investigates how offshore financial centres and tax haven jurisdictions shape, distort and challenge the established frameworks of international trade law. Supervised by Dr. İdil İnal.

    International trade law · Tax havens · WTO
